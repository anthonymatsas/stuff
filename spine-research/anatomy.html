02

Load on the L3 disc

By position · classic biomechanics

These numbers come from Nachemson's intradiscal pressure studies and modern biomechanics modeling. Disc pressure varies enormously by position — the difference between a "good" and "bad" lifting technique can be many hundreds of pounds of compressive force on the discs. Standing upright is the baseline reference (100%).

Lying on back

The lowest sustainable load on your discs. Roughly 25% of standing load. This is why mornings often feel best — your discs have been decompressed overnight. (And also why disc bulges are sometimes more provocable in the morning, when discs are most hydrated.)

~75lbs
Relative to standing: 25%
Sources & caveats Numbers approximated from Nachemson AL (1981) intradiscal pressure studies, Wilke et al. (1999) in vivo measurements, and McGill SM (2007) compression force modeling. Standing baseline ≈ 300 lbs / ~1400 N of compressive force on the L3 disc for an average adult — values shown above scale that. Real loads depend on body weight, lifted weight, lever arm, posture, muscle activation, and disc geometry. Use these as relative comparisons, not personal predictions. The point is the pattern: posture and lever-arm length dominate disc load, often more than the absolute weight being lifted.
